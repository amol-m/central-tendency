What is an average?

Imagine your teacher asks, "How tall is a typical person in this class?" Nobody wants to hear all thirty heights. They want one number that describes the whole group.

That one number is called a measure of central tendency. It's a fancy name for "where the middle of the data is". In everyday life we usually just call it an average.

There are three main kinds, and each finds the "middle" in its own way:

  • Meanthe fair share
  • Medianthe middle one
  • Modethe most common

Mean The fair share

The mean is what each value would be if everything were shared out equally.

How to find it

  1. Add them up. Find the total of all the values.
  2. Divide by how many. Divide the total by the number of values.

Worked example

Maya's football team scored 2, 4, 4, 5, 10 goals in five matches.

Add them up: 2 + 4 + 4 + 5 + 10 = 25

Divide by how many: 25 ÷ 5 = 5

The mean is 5 goals per match.

Think of it like this: if the team had scored exactly the same number in every match, and still got 25 in total, they'd have scored 5 each time.

Median The middle one

The median is the middle value when all the values are put in order.

How to find it

  1. Put them in order, from smallest to largest.
  2. Find the middle. If there are two middle values, the median is halfway between them (add them and divide by 2).

Worked example: an odd number of values

Five friends spent 25, 40, 15, 30, 20 minutes on homework.

In order: 15, 20, 25, 30, 40

The median is 25 minutes.

Worked example: an even number of values

Six cousins are 4, 12, 7, 9, 15, 6 years old.

In order: 4, 6, 7, 9, 12, 15

There are two middle values, so find halfway: (7 + 9) ÷ 2 = 8

The median is 8 years old, even though nobody is actually 8.

Mode The most common

The mode is the value that appears most often.

How to find it

  1. Count how many times each value appears.
  2. Pick the winner. The value with the highest count is the mode.

Worked example

Seven students have shoe sizes 5, 6, 6, 7, 6, 8, 5.

Size 5 appears 2 times, size 6 appears 3 times, sizes 7 and 8 appear once.

The mode is size 6.

A data set doesn't always have exactly one mode:

  • More than one mode. In 1, 1, 4, 4, 6 both 1 and 4 appear twice, so there are two modes: 1 and 4.
  • No mode. In 2, 5, 8 every value appears once, so nothing is more common than anything else. There is no mode.

The mode is the only average that works with words, not just numbers. You can find the most popular fruit, but you can't add up bananas and divide!

How to remember which is which

  • Median is in the middle, like the median strip down the middle of a road.
  • Mode sounds like MOst: it's the value you see the most.
  • Mean is the "mean" one, because it makes you do the most work: add them all, then divide.

Which average should I use?

All three are "averages", but they can give very different answers. Pick the one that tells the most honest story about your data.

Mean

Good at: using every single value, so nothing is ignored.

Watch out: one very big or very small value (an outlier) can drag it a long way.

Use it for: test scores in a class, or average temperature over a week.

Median

Good at: ignoring outliers, so it shows what's typical.

Watch out: it doesn't notice how big the extreme values are.

Use it for: house prices, or pocket money when one friend gets loads.

Mode

Good at: working with categories like colours or foods.

Watch out: there might be no mode, or several.

Use it for: the most popular shoe size a shop should stock.
